Market breadth measures how many stocks rose — not how far the headline index moved. When more than half the constituents advance, the market's strength is broad. When the figure sinks below fifty, a narrowing handful of heavyweights is carrying the tape while the rank and file quietly retreat.

The gauge above counts the share of the entire IDX listing — roughly 950 stocks — that closed higher today. Below, the panels trace the index itself, its breadth, and its momentum back through the years.

Fig. I

The Index Itself

IHSG · daily open-high-low-close

The headline index, one candle per session: the body spans open to close, the wick the day's full range. Green closed up, red closed down. The IHSG is weighted by company size, so a handful of the largest constituents move it — which is exactly what the breadth panels below are here to check it against.

Fig. II

The Breadth Line

% Advancing · 20 / 100 / 200-day moving averages · 50% neutral

The share of trading companies that closed higher, counted one vote each — a bank worth billions and a micro-cap count the same. Above the 50% line more stocks rose than fell. Single sessions are noisy, so the faint dots are the raw daily readings and the lines are their 20, 100 and 200-day averages. Green above the neutral line, red below.

Fig. III

The Zweig Breadth Thrust

10-day EMA of % Advancing · a climb from 40% to 61.5% inside ten sessions

Zweig's test asks whether a bottom was bought by the whole market or only by a handful of heavyweights: the ten-day average of advancing stocks must lift out of the oversold band and clear 61.5% within ten sessions. Jakarta has managed it —, marked ▲ on the axis, against a peak reading of —. Read the early triggers with care: the exchange listed barely 390 companies then against roughly 800 today, and a thinner market swings wider, so it clears the bar more easily than the same tape would now.

Fig. IV

Market Momentum — Stochastic

IHSG weekly %K / %D (15,3,3) · <20 oversold (buy zone), >80 overbought

Where this week's close sits inside the index's own range over the last fifteen weeks: 100% means it finished at the top of that range, 0% at the bottom. %K is the raw reading, %D its three-week average. The bands mark the extremes — below 20 the index is oversold, above 80 overbought. This one tracks price, not breadth, and runs on weekly bars, so it needs a long reading period to say anything.

Fig. V

Shinohara Intensity Ratio

IHSG weekly (26), Yahoo formula · the Weak − Strong spread

Shinohara measures buying and selling pressure as two ratios; the reading is the gap between them, so the gap itself is plotted. Above 100 — the shaded band — the market is extremely oversold, a rare and historically bullish reading.
